What you’re looking at

The excitation–minus–inhibition account (Spence)

Training S+ builds an excitatory gradient peaked on S+; training S− (no reward) builds an inhibitory gradient peaked on S−. Your responding to any patch is roughly excitation minus inhibition. Because the inhibitory dip sits on the S− side, it drags the net peak away from S− — landing it on a patch that was never rewarded during training.

That’s the counterintuitive part: the animal (or student) responds most to something it was never trained on. Simple “it learned to respond to S+” can’t explain it. Guttman & Kalish (1956); Hanson (1959); Spence (1937).
