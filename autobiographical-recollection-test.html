The Autobiographical Recollection Test

People differ not only in what they remember but in how remembering feels. Some relive the past as if traveling back into it; others recall the same events as flat summaries. This short self-report measures seven of those qualities.

Read each statement and rate how well it describes the way you remember events from your past. Answer honestly, thinking about your past-event memories in general. 1 means strongly disagree, 7 means strongly agree.

Your seven memory qualities

Each quality is the sum of three items, so scores range from 3 to 21. Higher means that quality is more prominent in how you remember. There is no good or bad profile here, only differences worth noticing.

Take it further

  • Do these scores match how you would describe thinking back to a recent birthday or a conversation from last week?
  • Would your profile look the same for a happy memory and a painful one, or would some qualities shift?
  • Chapter 15 asks what consciousness is for. If two people share an experience but score very differently here, what does that say about how private the act of remembering really is?
  • How might someone who scores high on reliving and vividness rely on their memory differently from someone who scores low, say, as a witness, a storyteller, or a student studying for an exam?
